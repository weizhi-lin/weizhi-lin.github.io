## How to Choose a Topic for Your Final Project (UG and MS students)

- This guide is intended for students who would like to work with me on a capstone or final-year project, such as ISE 298. It is not intended for students seeking research project opportunities. 
- If you would like to work with me on your final-year project, please review this guide carefully and identify a few potential project topics or directions before reaching out. 
- Please include a brief project proposal and your CV in the email you send me.

### For Students Interested in Industry

_- for students who already have a target job, role, or career direction in mind._

1. Review job descriptions
   - Look for common skills, tools, methods, and industry needs in relevant job postings.
2. Summarize common themes
   - Use AI tools to identify recurring requirements, techniques, and keywords, such as monitoring, diagnosis, prediction, optimization, simulation, or data analysis.
3. Choose a specific application domain
   - Select a focused industry area, such as healthcare operations, manufacturing, automotive systems, logistics, or supply chain.
4. Develop research questions
   - Formulate questions inspired by industry needs, such as how to monitor a process, diagnose problems, predict outcomes, or optimize cost, quality, or efficiency.
5. Discuss with the advisor for finalization. 

### For Students Interested in a Ph.D.

1. Identify your dream programs
   - Make a list of graduate schools, colleges, or departments you may want to apply to for a Ph.D.
   - Consider program reputation, research strengths, faculty expertise, location, funding opportunities, and fit with your long-term goals.
2. Search relevant departments and research groups
   - Visit the websites of the departments, labs, and research centers that match your interests.
   - Pay attention to their active research areas, recent publications, ongoing projects, and Ph.D. student profiles.
3. Identify potential advisors
   - Look for faculty members whose research aligns with your interests.
   - Review their personal websites, lab pages, Google Scholar profiles, and recent papers to understand their research directions.
4. Study the advisor’s research interests
   - Summarize the advisor’s main research topics, methods, applications, and recent publications.
   - Try to identify what research questions they are currently working on and what skills may be useful for joining their group.
5. Develop a research topic aligned with your Ph.D. goals
   - Formulate a project topic that connects with your potential Ph.D. direction.
   - The topic should help you build relevant research experience, strengthen your application, and prepare for future graduate study.
